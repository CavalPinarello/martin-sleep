Lumos

I'm building Lumos, a social robot. It starts with loneliness, as a companion for older adults who live alone, especially at night. Later the same robot can sit with a seven-year-old over homework, or keep a meeting on track. Loneliness comes first.

Code on GitHub

What I built

I built the prototype myself, by hand, at home in California, and I talk to it every day. It holds a conversation, remembers what you told it last week, and knows whose face and voice it's dealing with.

It also moves while it talks. It turns toward whoever is speaking, leans in when you tell it something, and goes still when you go quiet. A voice in a box can't do that, and it's what makes Lumos feel like someone in the room.

The pictures on this page are design renders of the version I'm working toward.

How it works

  1. 01EarsA microphone array hears who is talking and from which side, so it turns to the right person.
  2. 02EyeA camera recognises the people it lives with and greets them by name.
  3. 03MemoryIt keeps a long-term memory of a person's life: names, plans, what worried them on Tuesday.
  4. 04A mind at homeA small model fine-tuned for Lumos runs in the house. Harder questions go to larger models, and the robot doesn't go dead when a server does.
  5. 05BodyEvery reply comes with a movement, a turn or a nod or a lean, and at night a slow settle.
  6. 06NightAfter dark it drops to a whisper with no light, for the hours when there's nobody else to talk to.

What's next

First, the homes of people who live alone. I'll count success in hours spent with other people, not hours spent with the robot.
