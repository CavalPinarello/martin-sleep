Stanford research · Sardinia

BlueNotte

How do people who live past a hundred sleep? At Stanford I investigate sleep and longevity, and BlueNotte, the study I work on, takes the question to Sardinia.

Stanford profileCode on GitHub

How I work there

Sardinia has one of the highest densities of centenarians on Earth, with almost as many men as women among them, which is rare. Most of what anyone knows about how they sleep comes from questionnaires.

BlueNotte measures it. With our partners at the hospital in Alghero, we visit people at home and record their nights with sensors they barely notice, a ring or a watch and a monitor under the mattress, then ask them about their days. Stanford does the design and the analysis.

In Sardinia I see first-hand how rarely the oldest people are left alone. Lumos started there.

How it works

  1. 01VisitAt home, in the village, with the local hospital team.
  2. 02SensorsA ring or watch, and a monitor under the mattress.
  3. 03InterviewDays, meals, naps and the people around them.
  4. 04AnalysisAt Stanford, across sites.

What's next

Okinawa, with the University of the Ryukyus Hospital, so we can compare two long-lived populations with very different genes and diets.
